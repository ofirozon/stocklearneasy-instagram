## bull-bear

> Why a bull, and why a bear?
>
> Two animals run the language of the stock market, and almost nobody explains
> where they came from. A bull throws its horns upward. A bear swipes its paw
> down. That is the whole difference, and it is why a rising market is bullish
> and a falling one is bearish.
>
> Save this one. It is the vocabulary every other market story is written in.

## correction

> Ten percent, or twenty?
>
> The same red screen gets two different names. A fall of about ten percent is
> called a correction, and historically it is the common one. Twenty percent or
> more from the peak is where the label changes to a bear market.
>
> The number is not a prediction, it is just the dividing line between the two
> words you keep hearing.

## volatility

> Why does the price jump around so much?
>
> Up on Monday, down on Tuesday, and nothing about the company changed. Day to
> day, buyers and sellers pull the same line in opposite directions, and that
> argument is what volatility measures.
>
> Step back far enough and the same data stops looking like noise and starts
> looking like a trend. Same line. Different zoom.

## compound

> Slow for years. Then fast.
>
> Compounding is the quietest idea in investing and the one people give up on
> first, because for a long time the line barely leaves the ground.
>
> What changes is that the gains start earning gains of their own. The curve
> does not get a sudden push, it just bends, and the bend is the whole lesson.
