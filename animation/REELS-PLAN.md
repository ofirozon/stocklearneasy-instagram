# Turning the page into a reels page

Research and plan, 1.10.2026. Ofir's brief, by voice: recurring characters with
a defined look ("ג'ינומה של איך הקרקטר הולך להיראות") so they can act against
each other; the animated films become the reels; the current reels are not good
enough and get replaced. He asked for real research first and a written plan
after it.

---

## 1. What the research actually says

### The three seconds decide everything

Watch time is Instagram's top-ranked signal (Mosseri, confirmed 2026). 71% of
short-form viewers decide inside three seconds, and average short-form
retention sits near 1.5 seconds. When 30–50% of viewers leave between second 1
and second 3 the curve shows a cliff, and the algorithm reads the whole video
as low value **however good the rest is**.

The rule that follows is blunt: *the opening frame is the hook, not an
introduction to the hook.* Cold-open on the most visually loaded moment, then
backtrack. Cutting pre-rolls and title cards is the single most reliable lever
on average view duration.

**Our film fails this.** Seconds 0–2.6 are a kicker, a title and two animals
strolling in. The first thing that happens happens at second 7.75.

### The loop is worth more than a shorter film

This corrects what I told Ofir earlier today. I said 15 seconds, on completion
rate. With a loop the picture changes:

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Average Shorts retention | 73% |
| Looping content | can exceed 100% (rewatched in-session) |
| Target completion, sub-30s | above 60% |
| Replay rate: excellent / rare | 10% / 20% |
| **Optimal length for a loop** | **20–25s** |
| Loops weaken beyond | 35s |

Our 20.5s is already in the right band. It should stay there and become a loop,
not be cut to 15s. Five mechanics make a loop work: the opening frame poses the
question the ending revisits; a callback phrase echoes the hook's exact words;
the final frame matches the opening frame in background and angle; audio runs
continuously across the seam; and there is no static freeze, no 3-second CTA,
no silence at the restart point.

Our ending does the opposite of every one of those.

### Most people will never hear it

85–88% of reels are watched muted. Burned-in subtitles beat Instagram's
auto-captions by 2.5x and no captions by 3.4x, lift retention ~38%, and add up
to 40% more views.

Our narration carries the entire lesson and nothing is subtitled.

### A third of our frame is covered by Instagram's own UI

Meta publishes the safe area as percentages: 14% top (~270px), 35% bottom
(~670px), 40% at the bottom right (~770px), 6% each side (~65px). The usable
rectangle on a 1080x1920 frame is **roughly 950 x 980, from y 270 to y 1250**.

Measured against it, today's film:

| Element | y | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| kicker | 196 | under the username / audio header |
| title, scenes 2 and 3 | 212 | under the header |
| title, scene 1 | 262 | on the line |
| sub | 372 | safe |
| notice board | 572–1250 | safe, bottom edge exactly on the limit |
| **app strip** | **1636** | **hidden behind the caption stack** |
| **"STOCK LEARN EASY" footer** | **1806** | **fully hidden** |

The one element in the whole film aimed at app installs is invisible in the
place it will actually be watched. This is the most embarrassing finding in the
research and the cheapest to fix.

### Why animation is the right bet

Not because it is pretty. Infographic-style explainers are **saved at 3x the
rate** of talking-head finance content, and saves and shares are what Instagram
converts into distribution. Explainers also have a long tail, earning views for
months, where news posts die the same day. Animation stands out structurally in
a feed of talking heads, needs nobody on camera, and — the part that matters
here — can be generated by code.

Recurring characters are the compounding asset. A character the viewer
recognises turns single posts into a series, and a series is what brings people
back. This is exactly Ofir's instinct, and the research supports it.

### Audio

Trending audio carries a 29–33% reach advantage, but it fights a narrated
explainer. Original voiceover is the correct choice here; faceless
voiceover-driven content loops more invisibly because there is no physical
reset cue, and ~58% of faceless creators reported higher retention. A quiet
music bed under the narration is worth adding, mostly so the loop seam has
continuous audio.

### The uncomfortable part

Accounts under 10k followers average about **5,200 reach per reel**. This
account gets 1 to 5. That is three orders of magnitude below the benchmark, and
no change to the films closes that gap on its own. Two posts a day at identical
clock times, every day since launch, entirely through the API, with no human
activity on the account, is the pattern the platform reads as automation.

I have said this once before and I am not going to keep raising it. It belongs
in the plan because the plan is "turn this into a reels page", and the schedule
is part of that. Recovery guidance is consistent across sources: drop to one
post a day, vary the posting time, and give it 2–6 weeks.

### Where I am not certain

I could not find a credible head-to-head measurement of animated versus
live-action reel performance. The only nearby figure (54% of marketers make
live action, 24% animation) describes what gets produced, not what works.
Everything else above is sourced. Treat the "animation wins" claim as resting
on the saves multiplier and on structural reasoning, not on a direct A/B.

## 2. The plan

### Phase 1 — fix the frame (this is small, and it is most of the value)

1. **Cold open.** Kill the title card. Open on the bull already mid-throw with
   the price line already climbing, hold about a second, and only then let the
   title arrive. The question the film answers has to be on screen at frame 1.
2. **Loop it.** Last frame matches the first: same angle, same calm twilight,
   same two animals. The closing line becomes a callback to the opening words.
   No freeze, no dead air at the seam.
3. **Burn in the narration** as word-grouped captions, in the safe band, in DM
   Sans, with the same treatment every episode.
4. **Move everything into y 270–1250.** The app strip comes up out of the
   caption stack; the footer either moves up or goes, since the app strip says
   the same thing better.
5. **Add a music bed** under the voice, quiet, continuous across the loop.

### Phase 2 — the character bible

A real one, committed as `CHARACTERS.md` plus an SVG parts library, built on
how model sheets actually work:

- **Invariants, the never-change list:** head shape and proportions, eye shape
  and spacing, horn and ear silhouette, the scarf as the signature accessory,
  the exact palette, body-to-leg ratio.
- **Shape language**, already half-right by accident and worth making explicit:
  the bull is built from circles and upward diagonals, the bear from heavier
  rounded rectangles and downward diagonals. Optimism against weight, before a
  single word is spoken.
- **A turnaround**: side, 3/4 front, front. Today both characters only exist in
  profile, which is why they can never look at each other or at the viewer.
  This is the single biggest limit on "acting against each other" and it is
  what Ofir actually asked for.
- **Silhouette test**: fill the pose black. If you cannot tell what the animal
  is doing, the pose is rejected. Enforce it as a check script over rendered
  stills, the same way `card_check.py` guards the cards.
- **A pose library**: named poses (idle, walk, rear-up, swipe, point, shrug,
  look-at-other) as data, so an episode is written as a list of beats rather
  than hand-tuned transforms.

### Phase 3 — the episode generator

`episode.py <topic>` producing a 20–25s looped reel:

- one topic in, one film out, same village, same two characters
- beat sheet as data: hook pose, the turn, the payoff, the callback
- the price line on the notice board driven by `market_data.py`, so the chart
  is real when the topic has a ticker
- captions generated from the narration script, so they can never drift
- `--voice-only` and `--still` already exist and stay

### Phase 4 — the schedule

One reel a day, not two posts. Varied times. The carousels drop to a supporting
role. Reels are 60% of a healthy 2026 mix, and for an account this size reels
are the only surface with meaningful non-follower reach.

### Order of work

Phase 1 is a few hours and fixes defects that are costing views today. Phase 2
is the part Ofir asked for and unlocks everything after it. Phase 3 only pays
off once Phase 2 exists. Phase 4 is a one-line change to the schedule and
should happen alongside Phase 1.
